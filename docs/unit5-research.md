The running case is LineLab skate park. Ramp profiles are mathematical teaching models rather than engineering specifications. Trigonometric derivative and integral rules use radians. No induction, integration by parts, differential equations or other HL-only calculus is taught.

Numeric checks: squared-ramp gradient at x=3 is 6; polynomial gradient at x=2 is 16; the squared-ramp tangent at (1,1) is y=2x−1 and normal is y=−x/2+3/2; integrating 4x with height 5 at x=1 gives C=3; derivative of (3x+1)² at x=1 is 24; second derivative of x³−3x at x=2 is 12; maximum rectangular area with perimeter 20 m is 25 m²; velocity 2t−4 over [0,3] gives displacement −3 m and distance 5 m; between 2x and x² on [0,2], area is 4/3.

The numerical quiz questions use only arithmetic and powers, so POWER_KEYS is sufficient. Paper 1 practice should be attempted by hand despite the optional practice calculator. The full topic includes calculator-supported exploration, but these lessons are a compact study guide, not a replacement for classroom teaching and the official course materials.
